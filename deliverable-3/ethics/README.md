# COMP3180 A3 user study: ethics requirement, decision path and timeline

Prepared 14 Oct 2026 for Raphael Byun. All web pages below were retrieved on 14 Oct 2026. This is research and a draft, not advice from Macquarie. The decision belongs to the convenor and the Ethics Secretariat.

## 1. Bottom line

1. **You almost certainly need a decision from Macquarie before you recruit or run any session.** Under Macquarie policy and the National Statement, approval (or a written exemption or "not research" decision) must come before the research starts, and retrospective approval is not accepted. Recruiting at the Week 9-10 pracs is part of starting. If you have already approached people about the study itself, tell the convenor now.
2. **The study is human research on the plain definition.** It observes people, takes think-aloud notes and records their answers. That is "research conducted with or about people", with participants "being observed by researchers" and "taking part in surveys, interviews" (Macquarie Human Research Ethics Policy, cl 3).
3. **The likely pathway is not a full HREC.** Under the 2023 National Statement the risk is probably "low" (discomfort only, perhaps "minimal"), and low-risk research can be reviewed by a non-HREC pathway. Macquarie has low-risk subcommittees (the pages I could find are for the Faculty of Medicine, Health and Human Sciences; I do not know which one would take a computing study).
4. **Unknown, and the main question for the convenor:** whether COMP3180 has a unit-level (coursework or teaching-unit) ethics approval that the convenor lodges for all students; or whether you must be a Co-Investigator on an application with a staff Chief Investigator (the convenor); or whether the Ethics Secretariat will say this is outside the policy.
5. **Timeline is the real risk.** Pilot is from 19 Oct (5 days away), A3 is due 8 Nov. One MQ page I found (search summary) says a low-risk subcommittee aims to respond within 20 working days. Twenty working days from today lands on about 11 Nov, after the due date. See section 5.

### 2c. Inference (mine, not stated by any page)
- **Exemption under 5.1.17(c) probably does not fit.** Your study is not "for training purposes only", and your outcomes go into an assessed report and possibly a public repo, so they are not "for program use only". It might fit if the convenor says the whole study is a teaching exercise whose results go nowhere beyond the unit. Ask; do not assume.
- **The 25 per cent clause (M8) suggests the Policy's own student-research scope may not cover a single undergraduate unit.** That does not remove the duty to get approval: the FAQ says undergraduate research with participants needs approval (M11, search summary), and a unit-level route probably exists. Whether the Policy applies to COMP3180 A3 is a question for the Secretariat or convenor.
- **Your study is probably "low" rather than "minimal"**, because thinking aloud and being tested can cause mild discomfort, and your peers are the participants. Treat it as low risk.
- **Nothing in the plan points to greater-than-low risk:** no payment, no children or people with impaired capacity, no health data, no sensitive topics, no focus on Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people.
- **Teammates as proxies are still participants.** Using a teammate in a session is still human research.

## 3. Decision path

1. Is this research? Yes on the Policy definition (M1). Ask the convenor whether the unit treats it as a teaching activity (N5, M11).
2. Is there already a unit-level approval? If yes, get its number and conditions and fit your protocol under it.
3. If not, who is Chief Investigator? Probably the convenor, with you as Co-Investigator (M10). Ask whether they will sponsor.
4. Which pathway: exemption (N6), low-risk review, or full HREC? Expect low-risk review.
5. Do you need ethics training first? Yes (M3). Ask which module.
6. What documents does the reviewer need? Application, participant information and consent form, recruitment text, the six-item comprehension sets (Appendix B), the ten-need sheet, the think-aloud prompt (M11).
7. Do not start any recruitment or sessions until you hold written approval, or written confirmation that none is needed.

**Fallback, said plainly.**
- Running the comparison on yourself, on an AI model, on made-up data, or with non-human checks (such as the fidelity check against commit diffs) is **not a substitute** for the human-participant study the A3 brief calls for, as I understand the brief. I did not re-read the brief for this task, so confirm against it.
- What you can still do without participants, and should do anyway: finish the fidelity check, finalise the instruments (Appendix B items, ten-need sheet, think-aloud prompt), complete the ethics application, risk assessment and data plan, and write the study up as designed with a clear statement of why it did not run.
- **Ask the convenor** what they will accept: (a) a short extension on the study component; (b) assessment of the full protocol and ethics package if the study cannot run; or (c) a fast route (a unit-level approval or a Secretariat "not research" decision) to run a cut-down version, for example the five-reader floor only, no think-aloud, no audio ever. A cut-down study is a design change, so the convenor should agree to it.
- Do not run "informal" sessions and call them something else to avoid review. Retrospective approval is not available (M2, N4), and data collected first could be unusable.
