# Proxy scenario (participant handout)

Status: **draft for the Week 11 pilot.** One page. **Give it to proxies** (participants who are not working producers), read it aloud with them, and leave it on the desk for the whole session. **Producers do not get this page**; they are briefed verbally with the same one-line context (`session-protocol.md` 2.1, the master). It is the same for both report versions.

Researcher note (do not print): the scenario must not mention scene or prefab names, what either version contains, or that there are two versions. It describes the role and the task only. Facts about SS3D are limited to its own repository description ("Space Station 3D, another remake of SS13, but with an extra D") and what both report versions show.

---

## Your role today

You are the **producer** on a small game team. You plan and track the team's work. You do not write code and you do not open the Unity project.

The team makes **SS3D (Space Station 3D)**, an open-source game made in Unity. It is a remake of Space Station 13, a multiplayer game in which players crew a space station. The team is made of volunteers who work at different times and in different countries.

You have **just joined the team**. You have not worked on any of the weeks you will read about, and you do not know the people involved.

## Your situation

Each week the team's version history is turned automatically into a one-page summary: **What changed this week.** Today you will read the summaries for two different weeks, one at a time.

For each week, imagine it is the following Monday morning. Before stand-up you need to:
- understand what the team did last week,
- know what to ask testers to check in the next build, and
- be ready to give a short update to the team and to the people the team reports to.

## Rules for today

- **Use only the page in front of you.** You cannot open the code, the task tracker, the chat, or ask the team.
- **If the page does not tell you something, say so.** "The report doesn't say" is a good answer when it is true. Guessing as if the page said it is not.
- Some terms on the page:
  - **Change:** one saved piece of work, with a short title written by the person who made it.
  - **File change:** one file touched by one change. A file edited twice counts twice.
  - **Scenes and prefabs:** the levels of the game, and the reusable game objects (doors, items, menus) placed in them.
  - **.meta files and data and settings:** Unity's own supporting files. You can ignore them.
- There is no trick and no time pressure beyond the session plan. Raphael is testing the pages, not you.
