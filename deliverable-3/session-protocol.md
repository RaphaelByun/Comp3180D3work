# Session protocol (45 minutes)

Status: **draft for the Week 11 pilot, not yet piloted.** Follows A2's study design: within-subjects, two SS3D weeks, week and order counterbalanced, 45-minute sessions, pilot excluded from the data.

Words in **bold quotes** are said as written. Everything else is for Raphael only.

## 1. Before the session

Materials, per participant (print the night before):
- [ ] Information sheet and consent form (`participant-info-and-consent.md`), two copies (one to keep)
- [ ] Proxy scenario (`proxy-scenario.md`), proxies only. Producers are not given it; they are briefed verbally with the one-line context in script 2.1
- [ ] The two reports for this participant's sequence (section 3), each with its matching item sheet (section A of the items file). Write the participant code on every sheet first.
- [ ] Ten-need sheet (section A of `ten-need-sheet.md`)
- [ ] Blank "stand-up draft" sheet (think-aloud readers only)
- [ ] Note sheet (section 5) and a stopwatch or phone timer
- [ ] The completed researcher marking key for the ten-need sheet (kept face down)

Print both versions of a report at the same font and size. Version B is longer because of its extra section; do not shrink it to fit one page.

Room: a quiet desk, Raphael beside the participant rather than opposite (easier for think-aloud, less like an exam).

## 2. Minute by minute

| Clock | Block | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 00:00–05:00 | Briefing and consent | Script 2.1 |
| 05:00–17:00 | Report 1 (12 min) | Script 2.2 |
| 17:00–29:00 | Report 2 (12 min) | Script 2.3 |
| 29:00–35:00 | Ten-need sheet (6 min) | Script 2.4 |
| 35:00–45:00 | Think-aloud and follow-up (10 min), six readers only | Script 2.5 and 2.6 |
| 35:00 or 45:00 | Close | Script 2.7 |

Start the stopwatch at 00:00 and leave it running. Write the clock time on the note sheet at the start and end of every block.

### 2.1 Briefing and consent (00:00–05:00)

**"Thanks for coming. I'm Raphael. This is part of my COMP3180 project. I've built a tool that writes a one-page summary of what changed in a game project each week, and I want to see how well a producer can use it. I'm testing the pages, not you."**

**"Here's the information sheet. Take a minute to read it."** (Wait.)

Consent check, asked one at a time, waiting for a yes to each:
- **"Do you have any questions about the sheet?"**
- **"You can stop at any time, and you don't have to give a reason. Is that clear?"**
- **"I'll only take written notes, no audio or video. Your sheets and my notes carry a code, not your name. Is that OK?"**
- **"Are you happy to take part?"**

If yes, the participant signs; Raphael keeps one copy and gives the other to the participant. If no at any point, thank them and end the session; record nothing.

Record on the note sheet whether the participant is a producer or a proxy (a non-producer standing in for one), as they arrive; do not decide it beforehand.

**"Your code for today is [code]."**

- **Proxy** (not a working producer): **"Here's your scenario."** Hand over `proxy-scenario.md`. **"Let's read it together."** Read it aloud and leave it on the desk. **"Any questions about your role?"** Answer only questions about the role or the scenario's term list.
- **Producer:** no handout. Say the same context in one line: **"The game is SS3D, an open-source Unity remake of Space Station 13, made by volunteers in different countries. Imagine you've just joined the team as its producer, it's Monday morning before stand-up, and you only have the page in front of you."** If they ask what a term on the pages means, read them only the matching line of the term list in `proxy-scenario.md`.

### 2.2 Report 1 (05:00–17:00)

Hand over the first report and its item sheet together.

**"This is the summary for one week. You have about twelve minutes. Read the page, then answer the six questions. You can look back at the page whenever you like. Use only what's on the page; if it doesn't tell you, write 'the report doesn't say'. Short answers are fine. Tell me when you're done."**

During the block: stay quiet. If asked about the content, say **"I can't help with that one, sorry. Just answer as you would at work."** If asked what a term means, point a proxy to the scenario's term list; read a producer the matching line of that list. Say nothing more.

At 15:00, if not finished: **"About two minutes left."** At 17:00: **"Let's stop there and move on."** Note any unanswered items. Collect the report and the sheet.

### 2.3 Report 2 (17:00–29:00)

Hand over the second report and its item sheet.

**"Same task, a different week. About twelve minutes again. Use only this page."**

Same rules, warnings at 27:00 and stop at 29:00. Collect the item sheet. **Keep report 2 aside** if this participant is a think-aloud reader; otherwise collect it.

### 2.4 Ten-need sheet (29:00–35:00)

Hand over the ten-need sheet. No reports on the desk.

**"This one is not about either week. It's about game teams in general. Read the top part, then for each question tick one box: could a tool that reads the team's records answer it fully, partly, or not at all? Then circle which record would help. There's no right answer; I want your view. About six minutes."**

Do not explain T, R or U beyond what the sheet says. At 34:00: **"One minute left."** Collect the sheet.

While the participant finishes, compare their ticks with the marking key (face down to them). Pick the three follow-up needs for 2.6: first, the items where their tick differs from the key, in sheet order; if fewer than three differ, add the lowest-numbered items not yet chosen. Write the three numbers on the note sheet. Never show or mention the key.

### 2.5 Think-aloud: stand-up draft (35:00–41:00)

Return report 2 and a blank stand-up draft sheet.

**"Last part. Imagine it's Monday and you're giving a two-minute stand-up update about this week, from this page alone. Please write the key points you'd say. While you work, say out loud whatever you're thinking: what you're reading, what you're looking for, what you're unsure about. Don't explain it to me; just think aloud as if you were alone. I'll stay quiet."**

Neutral prods, used only after about 15 seconds of silence, one at a time:
- **"Please keep talking."**
- **"What are you thinking now?"**
- **"What are you looking at?"**

Do not ask "why", do not agree, praise or correct, and do not answer questions about the page (use the line in 2.2). Note the clock time of every prod.

At 41:00: **"Thanks, let's stop the draft there."** Collect the draft and the report.

### 2.6 Follow-up: three classifications (41:00–45:00)

Hand back the ten-need sheet.

For each of the three chosen needs:
**"On question [n], you ticked [their answer]. Talk me through how you decided."**

Neutral prods, if needed:
- **"Can you say more about that?"**
- **"Which record, if any, did you have in mind?"**
- **"What would the record need to show for you to tick a different box?"**

Do not say whether their answer matches Raphael's marking. About 80 seconds per need.

### 2.7 Close

**"That's everything, thank you. One of the two pages you read had an extra section listing the scenes and prefabs that changed; I'm testing whether that section helps. Please don't tell classmates which questions or pages you saw until after Week 12, because they may still take part. Any questions for me?"**

Readers who do not do the think-aloud finish at 35:00 with the same close.

## 3. Counterbalancing

W1 = week of 8 Feb 2021 (`study/week1-2021-02-08`); W2 = week of 19 Jul 2021 (`study/week2-2021-07-19`). A = baseline (`report-A-baseline.md`), B = treatment (`report-B-treatment.md`). Each reader sees both weeks and both versions, never the same week twice.

| Sequence | Report 1 | Report 2 | Think-aloud draft uses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | W1-B | W2-A | W2-A (baseline) |
| S2 | W2-A | W1-B | W1-B (treatment) |
| S3 | W1-A | W2-B | W2-B (treatment) |
| S4 | W2-B | W1-A | W1-A (baseline) |

| Code | Sequence | Think-aloud | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P0 (pilot) | S1 | Yes | Excluded from data. S1 puts the longest page (W1-B) first, the hardest timing case. |
| R01 | S1 | Yes | |
| R02 | S2 | Yes | |
| R03 | S3 | Yes | |
| R04 | S4 | Yes | R01–R04 cover all four sequences once. |
| R05 | S2 | Yes | R05 + R06 pair (S2 + S1): think-aloud stays 3 baseline / 3 treatment and 3 W1 / 3 W2 |
| R06 | S1 | Yes | |

| R08 | S3 | No | Each sequence used twice over R01–R08. |

Codes and sequences go to participants **in order of arrival**, not by name. If someone withdraws, the next person takes the next unused row. The participant floor, and what happens below it, is an open decision stated once in `README.md` (open decision 5). Do not apply either reading until Raphael has chosen.

Report the per-week baseline scores (A2: week difficulty is a rival explanation).

## 4. What is recorded

| Data | Form | Kept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two item sheets | Participant's handwriting, coded | Yes |
| Ten-need sheet | Ticks and circles, coded | Yes |
| Stand-up draft | Participant's handwriting, coded (think-aloud readers) | Yes |
| Note sheet | Raphael's timestamped notes, coded | Yes |
| Block times | Start and end clock time of each block | Yes, on the note sheet |
| Audio or video | Not recorded | Not collected |
| Name ↔ code list | One separate sheet | Kept apart from all data (see consent form) |

No names on any data sheet. If a participant names a person or team aloud, write [name] or [team].

## 5. Note sheet format

Header: code, role (producer or proxy, recorded on arrival), sequence, date, start time, block times (5 boxes: brief, R1, R2, sheet, think-aloud).

One line per note, clock time from the running stopwatch:

```
[mm:ss] TAG  text
```

Tags:
- **TA** think-aloud words, verbatim in quotes as far as possible, e.g. `[36:12] TA "no idea what the meta ones are, skip"`
- **OBS** something seen, e.g. `[37:40] OBS flips back to the changes table`
- **PR** a prod Raphael gave, e.g. `[38:05] PR keep talking`
- **Q** a question the participant asked, and the reply
- **FU-n** follow-up on sheet item n, e.g. `[42:10] FU-7 "commit titles say what, not why"`
- **TIME** block overrun or unanswered item, e.g. `[17:00] TIME item 6 blank`

These notes are what the qualitative arm codes against Ko et al.'s (2007) 21 information types, so write what the reader says they need or cannot find, in their words.
